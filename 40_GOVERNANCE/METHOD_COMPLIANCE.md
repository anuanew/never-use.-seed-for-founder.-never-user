## THE FAILURES, NAMED RATHER THAN EXPLAINED AWAY

### 🔴 Rule 10 - RED across every roadmap
**None of these roadmaps has been through SPAN and KEEPER, because neither exists.** His method is
explicit that a roadmap is what comes out of a meeting between the roadmap wonder and the doctrine
auditor (`R-4.3`, `R-5.1`: *"you must birth the roadmap wonder"*).

So the honest status of this entire repository is: **a standing-in draft awaiting the audit it was
designed to receive.** Every file says so in its own header. **This is the single largest caveat here
and it is not a formality** - the alternative reading is that a coder wrote roadmaps and called them
his, which is the exact thing the corpus keeps catching.

### 🟠 Rule 6 - deferred items are not yet starred
His rule: *"It has a star next to it that says I can be deferred... Or the roadmap should just be
linkable. ACL is supposed to link everything."*

**Deferred items in these roadmaps are described in prose rather than carried as a marked state with an
ACL address.** They are not lost - every one is written down - but they are not *starred and linkable*
in the way he specified. **Closing this requires the ACL legend (GR Phase 0), so it is genuinely
blocked, not merely undone.**

### 🟠 Rule 4 - entry conditions thin in three roadmaps
`R4`, `R5`, and `R7` carry entry conditions on most phases but not all. Where a phase inherits its
entry condition from an existing estate roadmap (the F1-F5 order, the six faces), this repository
**did not restate it**, on the reasoning that restating creates a second truth store.

**That reasoning is defensible and it is still a gap** - a lane reading `R4` alone does not see every
gate. Fix: an explicit inherited-entry-condition line per phase.

### 🟠 Rule 7 - `R7` dependencies are thin
`R7` is mostly non-technical (a message, a purge, a roster, a business decision), and its dependencies
are largely **on him** rather than on other phases. The dependency table is correspondingly sparse.
**Named rather than papered over.**

### 🟠 Rule 1 - the proportion is under his stated shape
His shape is roughly **five phases with about twelve sub-phases each** (`R-1.2`). These roadmaps run
**six to nine phases with three to five sub-phases each.**

**That is genuinely thinner than he specified, in the sub-phase dimension.** Two honest readings:
1. His number describes a roadmap for a **known** build; several of these are audits and open
   questions, where inventing twelve sub-phases would be manufacturing detail - which is its own
   failure (a sentence claiming more than the floor does).
2. Or they are simply not decomposed enough yet, and SPAN's first job is to break them down.

**Reading 2 is more likely correct for `R1`, `R5`, and `R6`**, which describe real builds and could
carry more sub-phases honestly. Recorded as a deficiency to be worked, not as a defensible choice.
